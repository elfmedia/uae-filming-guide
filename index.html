Section 01

UAE Media Regulatory Matrix: Federal & Emirate-Level Jurisdictions

Filming commercially in the United Arab Emirates operates under a two-tiered legal framework: Federal Media Law (UAE Media Council / NMC) governing content standards, religious/cultural sensitivities, and commercial trade licensing, and Emirate-Specific Film Commissions governing physical location permits, road closures, and municipal services.

Under Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 on the Regulation of Media, all entities conducting photographic, cinematographic, or television broadcasting operations must operate under a valid commercial media license with registered activities. Foreign production companies cannot independently petition municipal authorities without an accredited UAE Production Service Company serving as the statutory applicant.

Abu Dhabi Film Commission (ADFC) 30%+ Cash Rebate Framework

The Abu Dhabi Film Commission offers the Middle East’s premier production incentive: a 30% cash rebate (scaling up to 35% for qualifying cultural/strategic initiatives) on all qualifying spend incurred in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi.

Qualifying productions include feature films, television drama series, commercial advertisements, documentaries, and interactive video game cinematics.

Section 03

Dubai Film and TV Commission (DFTC) Commercial Permitting

In Dubai, all public filming requires a formal permit issued via the DFTC portal. The permit system categorizes shoots into Public Locations (beaches, parks, desert highways), Government Entities (Dubai Police, RTA roads, Dubai Health Authority), and Private Property.

Crucial Dubai Legal Nuance: Private Property Filming

Even when filming inside a private residence, luxury villa, hotel, or private business in Dubai, a DFTC "Private Location Notification" or baseline permit is still legally required if the content is intended for commercial broadcast, paid digital advertising, or corporate distribution. Filming without this permit exposes the brand and producer to immediate administrative fines (AED 10,000 - 50,000) and equipment impoundment.

Equipment Logistics: ATA Carnet, TDRA Wireless Approvals & Battery Safety

For international directors, cinematographers, and production companies flying into Abu Dhabi (AUH) or Dubai (DXB) with cinema packages (ARRI ALEXA 35, RED V-Raptor, Cooke/Angenieux glass), proper customs handling is essential to prevent costly customs bonds or impoundment at port of entry.

ATA Carnet Customs Passport

The UAE accepts ATA Carnets issued by national Chambers of Commerce. Gear entered on an ATA Carnet enters duty-free and VAT-free for up to 12 months.

Important: Every serial number on the manifest must strictly match physical units. Any discrepancy triggers customs delays.

TDRA Wireless Frequency Clearance

Wireless video transmitters (Teradek Bolt 4K, DJI Transmission) and wireless lavalier audio systems (Lectrosonics, Wisycom) operating outside standard consumer Wi-Fi bands require TDRA type-approval or temporary event spectrum allocation.

Public Liability Insurance & International Crew Visas

Section 09

Frequently Asked Compliance Questions

Can an international crew shoot in Dubai or Abu Dhabi on a visit visa? ▼
No. Filming commercially on public land or with cinema equipment while on a tourist or visit visa violates UAE labor and immigration regulations. Foreign directors, DPs, and technical crew must be sponsored under a temporary commercial project permit or mission work visa facilitated by an accredited UAE media house like elf media.
How far in advance must commercial drone permits be filed? ▼
Standard lead time is 7 to 10 working days for GCAA (Federal/Abu Dhabi) and 5 working days for DCAA (Dubai). If filming within 8km of aviation hubs or sensitive security corridors, inter-agency Ministry of Defence (MOD) security clearance can take up to 14 working days.
What are the legal penalties for filming without DFTC or ADFC permits? ▼
Penalties include immediate shutdown of production, administrative fines ranging from AED 10,000 to AED 100,000+, seizure and impoundment of digital media cards and camera hardware, and potential blacklisting of the production entity by the UAE Media Council.
